## 2. Theoretical & Methodological Verification

### 2.1 Target Definitions & Horizon Semantics
Annualized realized variance proxy across $H$ closed hourly candles is computed causally:
$$y_{t, H} = \text{RV}_{t, H}^{\text{ann}} = \frac{8,760}{H} \sum_{k=1}^H r_{t+k}^2, \quad \text{where } r_s = \ln\left(\frac{P_s}{P_{s-1}}\right)$$
- **1-Day Horizon ($H = 24\text{h}$):** $\text{rv24h\_var\_ann}$ represents 24 forward hourly candles. It is an hourly-sampled realized variance proxy, NOT a continuous tick-level integrated variance.
- **7-Day Horizon ($H = 168\text{h}$):** $\text{rv7d\_var\_ann}$ represents 168 forward hourly candles (7 calendar days). This is the primary target for weekly risk envelopes and C2 conformal bounds.
- **22-Day Horizon ($H = 528\text{h}$):** $\text{rv22d\_var\_ann}$ represents a standard institutional trading month (22 days $\times 24\text{h} = 528\text{h}$).

### 2.2 Point-in-Time Causal Boundary & Overlapping Alignment
- At forecast issue time $t_{\text{issue}} = t$, features utilize strictly closed backward candles:
  $$r_{t-k}^2 \quad \text{for } k \ge 0 \implies t_{\text{feature}} \le t_{\text{issue}}$$
- The target $y_t$ accumulates forward returns from $t+1$ to $t+H$. It cannot be observed or evaluated until $t_{\text{maturity}} = t+H$.
- **Serial Dependence Hazard:** Consecutive hourly forecasts $\hat{y}_t$ and $\hat{y}_{t+1}$ share $H-1$ common return periods. This induces an automatic Moving Average $\text{MA}(H-1)$ error process. Standard OLS standard errors and naive $t$-tests underestimate forecast error variance by orders of magnitude.

### 2.3 Parameter Fitting: OLS vs. QLIKE Quasi-Likelihood M-Estimation
- **Conventional OLS on Variance:**
  $$\min_\beta \sum_{t=1}^T (y_t - X_t \beta)^2$$
  OLS assumes additive Gaussian errors with constant variance. Under heteroskedastic financial returns, OLS heavily overweights high-volatility spike regimes, leading to severe distortion during tranquil regimes.
- **Log-OLS (Corsi 2009 canonical formulation):**
  $$\min_\beta \sum_{t=1}^T (\ln y_t - X_t \beta)^2, \quad \hat{y}_t = \exp(X_t \hat{\beta})$$
  Ensures positivity, but minimizes Mean Squared Error in log space. Patton (2011) proved that MSE on $\ln(y)$ is not loss-coherent if the downstream evaluation metric is QLIKE.
- **Loss-Coherent QLIKE M-Estimation (Patton 2011; Puke & Schweikert 2026):**
  $$L_{\text{QLIKE}}(y_t, \hat{y}_t) = \frac{y_t}{\hat{y}_t} - \ln\left(\frac{y_t}{\hat{y}_t}\right) - 1$$
  $$\min_\beta \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \left( \frac{y_t}{\exp(X_t \beta)} + X_t \beta \right) + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j^2$$
  First-order condition:
  $$\sum_{t=1}^T X_t \left( 1 - \frac{y_t}{\hat{y}_t} \right) = 0$$
  The parameter vector sets the mean relative forecast error orthogonal to regressors, directly minimizing expected out-of-sample QLIKE risk.

### 2.4 Positivity Constraints: Why Linear OLS Fails
In our audit of the 1,178 holdout bars under unconstrained linear OLS HAR-RS-DOW:
- **50 bars (4.24% of all holdout forecasts)** produced negative or zero variance ($\hat{y} \le 0$).
- Minimum unconstrained forecast: $\hat{y} = -0.0167$.
- When clipped to $10^{-6}$, the ratio $y / \hat{y}$ explodes to $\approx 50,000$, resulting in an average QLIKE of **$1,192.51$**!
- **Rule of Econometric Construction:** Linear OLS with unconstrained parameters must never be deployed for volatility forecasting. All operational volatility engines in BTCognitive must enforce exponential link functions $\hat{y} = \exp(X\beta)$ or strict non-negative manifold projections.

## 4. Modern External Literature Integration

### 4.1 Huang, Wang, and Liao (2026, *IRFA*, DOI: 10.1016/j.irfa.2026.105461)
- **Title:** *"Forecasting Bitcoin realized volatility under regime switching and delayed mean reversion"*
- **Core Contribution:**
  - Proposes a continuous-time framework combining latent Markov regime-switching with a distributed-delay Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process.
  - Documents that Bitcoin volatility exhibits **delayed mean reversion**: after sudden volatility expansions (liquidation cascades), volatility does not decay exponentially as standard AR(1) or HAR models assume; instead, decay exhibits a distinct structural delay followed by regime transition.
- **Econometric Relevance to BTCognitive:**
  - Explains why static HAR models degrade during volatile regimes: static linear coefficients force an identical speed of mean reversion regardless of whether the market is in a low-volatility accumulation regime or an active deleveraging phase.
  - Supports our rolling-window findings: a rolling 1,000-bar window achieves QLIKE $0.4324$ vs $0.4827$ for static OLS by partially adapting to regime transitions.

### 4.2 Wafi, El-Halaby, and Aboul-Dahab (2026, *Digital Finance*, DOI: 10.1007/s42521-026-00229-9)
- **Title:** *"Regime-dependent bitcoin volatility forecasting under alternative information sets: evidence from econometric, deep learning, and hybrid models"*
- **Core Contribution:**
  - Evaluates daily Bitcoin volatility across market regimes (Normal vs. Crisis) and alternative information sets: Technical (T), Technical + Sentiment (TS), and Technical + Sentiment + Blockchain (TSB) using QLIKE, RMSE, MAE, and Diebold–Mariano tests.
  - Concludes that predictability is **strictly regime-dependent**: during normal market conditions, parsimonious econometric models (GARCH, HAR) are highly competitive and cannot be displaced by complex deep learning models. Complex hybrid models only demonstrate outperformance during acute crisis regimes.
- **Econometric Relevance to BTCognitive:**
  - Validates our decision to maintain parsimonious models as the primary baseline.
  - Explains why adding day-of-week and semivariance features fails to deliver robust multi-horizon gains: outside of specific stress episodes, the additional parameters increase estimation variance without compensating variance reduction.
