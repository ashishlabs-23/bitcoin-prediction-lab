## 1. Executive Summary and Readiness Assessment

### 1.1 Executive Summary
Phase 1A successfully eliminated the hardcoded static telemetry numbers (`0.3980`, `0.5620`, `91.10`, `6.1420`, `5.28`, `-14.0`) and replaced empty-string placeholder cryptographic digests (`MODEL_HASH = sha256(b"")`, `FEATURE_SCHEMA_HASH = md5(b"")`) with SHA-256 digests derived directly from the physical artifact bytes (`har_rs_dow_v1.joblib`) and canonical schema representations.

However, an independent mathematical and forensic audit of the resulting telemetry pipeline reveals **critical scientific defects** that preclude interpreting current monitoring metrics as valid real-world empirical observations:

1. **Evidence Set Contamination (Test-Fixture Leakage):** Forensic inspection of the 71 post-repair records in `experiments/results/market_memory.db` proves that **100% of the sample consists of synthetic test fixtures** generated by unit test suites (`tests/test_live_forecast_resolution.py` and `tests/test_range_database_integration.py`). The elapsed time between forecast and resolution timestamps across all 71 rows is between **3.1 and 45.7 milliseconds**, featuring hardcoded test prices ($100,000 and $90,000). Not a single row represents genuine live out-of-sample market observations.
2. **`baseline_delta_bps` Lookahead Contamination:** The dynamically calculated baseline error in `engine/longitudinal_status.py` computes a "naive baseline" by taking the median of *future realized excursions* across the evaluation slice (`naive_mfe = df["actual_mfe"].median()`). This introduces severe in-sample lookahead bias rather than comparing against an independent, causal, pre-registered benchmark forecast.
3. **Miscalibration of `p90_coverage_pct` vs Joint Containment:** The metric labeled `p90_coverage_pct` measures joint path containment: $\mathbf{1}(\text{actual\_high} \le U \text{ and } \text{actual\_low} \ge L)$. For marginal 90% excursion quantiles, the theoretical joint coverage is bounded by $80\% \le P(\text{High} \le U \cap \text{Low} \ge L) \le 90\%$ (and $\approx 81\%$ under independence). Labeling joint containment as a 90% coverage metric and comparing it against a nominal 90% milestone is mathematically invalid without bivariate conformal joint calibration.
4. **Winkler Score Formulation Inconsistency:** The calculation in `longitudinal_status.py` averages two one-sided percentage quantile scores on excursions rather than computing the proper strictly scoring interval score on the scalar forecast target (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007). Across the codebase, three mutually contradictory Winkler formulations coexist.

## 5. In-Depth Metric Audits

### 5.1 MFE and MAE Errors (`mfe_error_pct`, `mae_error_pct`)
- **Mathematical Lineage:**
  At forecast generation (`engine/range_forecast_service.py`):
  $$\widehat{\text{mfe}}_{p50} = \text{round}(\exp(\widehat{\ln RV}) \times 0.45, 5)$$
  At outcome resolution (`engine/forecast_outcome_monitor.py:L81-85`):
  $$\text{act\_mfe} = \max\left(0, \frac{\text{act\_high}}{\text{current\_price}} - 1.0\right)$$
  $$\text{mfe\_error} = \text{act\_mfe} - \widehat{\text{mfe}}_{p50} \quad (\text{Signed Error in } \texttt{forecast\_outcomes})$$
  At aggregation (`engine/longitudinal_status.py:L142`):
  $$\text{mfe\_error\_pct} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |\text{mfe\_error}_i| \times 100$$
- **Audit Findings:**
  1. `mfe_error` in `forecast_outcomes` is a **signed error** (actual minus predicted).
  2. Aggregation in `longitudinal_status.py` computes the **mean absolute error** and multiplies by 100 to express it in percentage points.
  3. **Ambiguity:** Calling this metric `mfe_error_pct` suggests a relative percentage error $\frac{|y - \hat{y}|}{y}$, whereas it is actually the mean absolute deviation of return excursions.
  4. **Acronym Collision:** `mae_error_pct` stands for "Mean Absolute Error of the Maximum Adverse Excursion". In machine learning literature, MAE almost universally stands for Mean Absolute Error. This double acronym creates widespread confusion.
- **Recommendation:** Rename to `mean_abs_mfe_error_pct` and `mean_abs_mae_error_pct`.

### 5.2 `p90_coverage_pct` vs Joint Containment
- **Mathematical Lineage:**
  In `engine/forecast_outcome_monitor.py:L87-89`:
  $$\text{upper\_covered} = \mathbf{1}(\text{actual\_high} \le \text{upper\_p90})$$
  $$\text{lower\_covered} = \mathbf{1}(\text{actual\_low} \ge \text{lower\_p90})$$
  $$\text{path\_contained} = \text{upper\_covered} \land \text{lower\_covered}$$
  In `engine/longitudinal_status.py:L148`:
  $$\texttt{p90\_coverage\_pct} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \text{path\_contained}_i \times 100$$
- **Audit Findings:**
  1. The model predicts two one-sided boundaries: an upper barrier based on the 90th percentile of favorable excursion and a lower barrier based on the 90th percentile of adverse excursion.
  2. Let event $A = \{\text{actual\_high} \le U\}$ and event $B = \{\text{actual\_low} \ge L\}$. By construction, marginally $P(A) = 0.90$ and $P(B) = 0.90$.
  3. Under Fréchet-Hoeffding probability bounds:
     $$P(A \cap B) \ge P(A) + P(B) - 1 = 0.90 + 0.90 - 1 = 0.80$$
     $$P(A \cap B) \le \min(P(A), P(B)) = 0.90$$
     If $A$ and $B$ are independent, $P(A \cap B) = 0.90 \times 0.90 = 0.81$ (81%).
  4. **Scientific Invalidation:** It is mathematically impossible for a joint event $A \cap B$ constructed from separate marginal 90% quantiles to have an expected coverage of 90%. Comparing `path_contained` against a 90% milestone target is mathematically invalid.
- **Recommendation:** Immediately rename `p90_coverage_pct` to `joint_path_containment_pct`. If a true 90% two-sided prediction interval is desired, calibrate the marginal barriers at $1 - \alpha/2 = 95\%$ or apply bivariate conformal calibration.

### 5.3 Winkler Interval Score (`winkler_score`)
- **Mathematical Formulation:**
  Gneiting & Raftery (2007) establish the strictly proper scoring rule for a $(1 - \alpha)$ central prediction interval $[L, U]$ on scalar target $y$:
  $$IS_\alpha(L,U;y) = (U-L) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(L-y)\mathbf{1}(y<L) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(y-U)\mathbf{1}(y>U)$$
  In `engine/longitudinal_status.py:L158-164`:
  $$W_{\text{high}} = \text{width} + \frac{2}{\alpha} \max(0, \text{act\_mfe} - \widehat{\text{mfe}}_{p90})$$
  $$W_{\text{low}} = \text{width} + \frac{2}{\alpha} \max(0, \text{act\_mae} - \widehat{\text{mae}}_{p90})$$
  $$\texttt{winkler\_score} = \frac{W_{\text{high}} + W_{\text{low}}}{2} \times 100$$
- **Audit Findings:**
  1. The implementation does not score a scalar outcome $y$ against $[L, U]$. Instead, it treats the excursion envelope as two separate one-sided quantile forecasts, evaluates separate penalties on MFE and MAE breaches, averages them, and scales by 100.
  2. In `research/interval_sharpness.py:L107`, Winkler score is computed against `actual_closes` in raw dollars ($W \approx 605$). In `engine/longitudinal_status.py`, it is computed on percentage excursions ($W \approx 24.4$).
  3. These are completely different scales and mathematical objects.
- **Recommendation:** Formally document this metric as an **Excursion-Penalized Envelope Score** (`excursion_winkler_score`), and align units across research and production.

### 5.4 Baseline Delta (`baseline_delta_bps`) — Deep Forensic Analysis
- **Current Code in `engine/longitudinal_status.py:L166-168`:**
  ```python
  naive_mfe = df["actual_mfe"].median() if "actual_mfe" in df.columns and df["actual_mfe"].notnull().any() else 0.0
  naive_mfe_error = np.abs(df["actual_mfe"] - naive_mfe)
  baseline_delta_bps = round(float((df["mfe_error"].abs().mean() - naive_mfe_error.mean()) * 10000.0), 1)
  ```
- **Audit Findings:**
  1. **Lookahead Contamination:** `naive_mfe` is computed as `df["actual_mfe"].median()`. This is the median of future realized excursions over the entire evaluation sample. A trader or model at time $t$ cannot know the median excursion of the upcoming months.
  2. **Absent Benchmark Ledger:** No independent baseline predictions (e.g. 30-day rolling historical median excursion, 24-hour ATR benchmark, or random walk) are logged or queried in the SQLite database.
  3. **Sign Convention Ambiguity:** If `model_loss < baseline_loss`, the current subtraction `(model_error - naive_error)` produces a **negative** value. A negative delta indicates model superiority (error reduction), but without explicit documentation, consumers frequently assume positive numbers represent outperformance.
- **Recommendation:** **Immediately set `baseline_delta_bps = None` (null)** in `engine/longitudinal_status.py`. Mark this metric as **UNAVAILABLE** until a pre-registered causal benchmark forecast is persisted side-by-side with model predictions.

## 7. Statistical Dependence, Horizon Overlap, and Uncertainty Estimation

### 7.1 Temporal Autocorrelation in Rolling 24-Hour Windows
In financial time series, issuing 24-hour forecasts on hourly bars creates a **23-hour overlapping evaluation window**.
Adjacent forecast errors $\epsilon_t = Y_{t+24} - \widehat{Y}_{t+24}$ and $\epsilon_{t+1} = Y_{t+25} - \widehat{Y}_{t+25}$ share 23 common price return innovations:
$$\text{Cov}(\epsilon_t, \epsilon_{t+k}) \gg 0 \quad \text{for } |k| < 24$$

### 7.2 Invalidation of Independent Binomial Standard Errors
Applying the standard binomial confidence interval:
$$\text{SE}_{\text{iid}} = \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{N}}$$
assumes independent Bernoulli trials. Under strong positive temporal dependence, this formula **severely underestimates the true variance of empirical coverage** by a factor of 3x to 5x.

As proved by **Zhai, Cheng & Wu (2026)**, the asymptotic variance of empirical coverage $\hat{p}_N$ under temporal dependence is governed by the long-run variance:
$$\sigma_{\text{LR}}^2 = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \text{Cov}(\mathbf{1}(Y_t \in C_t), \mathbf{1}(Y_{t+k} \in C_{t+k}))$$
$$\sqrt{N}(\hat{p}_N - (1 - \alpha)) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{LR}}^2)$$

### 7.3 Effective Sample Size ($N_{\text{eff}}$) Formulation
In `research/post_repair_block_builder.py:L153-154`:
$$\rho = \max(0, \min(0.95, \text{lag1\_autocorr}))$$
$$N_{\text{eff}} = N \frac{1 - \rho}{1 + \rho}$$
While this AR(1) approximation accounts for first-order serial correlation, for a 24-hour horizon with hourly stepping, correlation extends through lag 24. A multi-lag Bartlett or Newey-West spectral variance estimator is required to prevent inflated statistical confidence.

### Identity vs. Provenance Distinction
The SHA-256 digest establishes **artifact identity** (the exact file bytes on disk have not been altered). It does **not** establish **artifact provenance** (e.g. training data hash, random seed, training timestamp, preprocessing pipeline digest). 
To reach full scientific provenance, the manifest must bind the model artifact hash to:
1. The exact SHA-256 of the training parquet dataset (`btc_1h_features.parquet`).
2. The scikit-learn pipeline parameter state.
3. The cryptographic git commit hash of the training script.
